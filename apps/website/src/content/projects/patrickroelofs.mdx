<Section id="problem" tone="coral" hint="01" label="The problem">
  <Statement>Handover took **45 minutes**, and most of it was spent scrolling.</Statement>

  Each patient had a dozen fields. Nurses printed the list, folded it into a pocket and added notes by hand, so the screen was out of date by the end of the first hour.
</Section>

<Section id="process" tone="navy" hint="02" label="How we got there">
  I started with three night shifts on Ward 4B, then interviewed forty nurses across four hospitals. Everyone printed the list. Nobody trusted the screen.

  From there we rewrote every patient card around one question: what does the next nurse need to do in the next hour? Everything else moved one tap away.

  ### Testing on the ward

  The pilot ran on two wards beside the old tool for twelve weeks. We asked nurses to read cards aloud while a call bell rang; anything they skipped, we cut.

  After nine months of rollout it was on 300 wards, with a training video under four minutes long.
</Section>
